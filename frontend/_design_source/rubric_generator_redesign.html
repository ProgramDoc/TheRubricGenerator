New England J. of Medicine · 2024 · Vol 390 No 12

Empagliflozin and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction

M. Anker, S. D. Butler, G. Filippatos, et al. — EMPEROR-Preserved Investigators

Abstract

Sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors reduce the risk of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure in patients with heart failure and a reduced ejection fraction, but their effects in patients with heart failure and a preserved ejection fraction are uncertain.

Design. Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. 5,988 patients with NYHA class II–IV heart failure and an ejection fraction of more than 40%.

In this double-blind trial, we randomly assigned 5,988 patients with class II to IV heart failure and an ejection fraction of more than 40% to receive empagliflozin (10 mg once daily) or placebo, in addition to usual therapy.

Methods

The primary outcome was a composite of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure. Secondary outcomes included total hospitalizations for heart failure and the rate of decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate.

Results

Over a median of 26.2 months, the primary outcome event occurred in 415 of 2,997 patients in the empagliflozin group (13.8%) and in 511 of 2,991 patients (17.1%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.79; 95% CI 0.69 to 0.90; P<0.001).

The effect was mainly related to a lower risk of hospitalization for heart failure in the empagliflozin group. Total hospitalizations for heart failure and cardiovascular death did not differ significantly between the groups.

Conclusions

Empagliflozin reduced the combined risk of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure in patients with heart failure and a preserved ejection fraction, regardless of the presence or absence of diabetes.
